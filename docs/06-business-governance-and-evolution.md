# Business, governance, and evolution questions

## Business model and economics

1. Who receives value, who pays, and are they the same party?
2. What behavior does the revenue model encourage or distort?
3. What pricing unit aligns with customer value and remains understandable?
4. What are acquisition, onboarding, service, infrastructure, support, compliance, and retention costs?
5. How do costs behave at low, expected, and extreme scale?
6. What contribution margin or cost-saving threshold makes the product sustainable?
7. Which customers, features, or usage patterns create disproportionate cost or risk?
8. What procurement, billing, refund, tax, fraud, and revenue-recognition requirements exist?
9. What assumptions drive the business case, and how sensitive is it to change?
10. What funding and staffing are required to operate the product through its expected life?

## Funding viability and financial failure

F1. Which source—revenue, internal budget, grant, debt, or investment—is committed, conditional, forecast, or merely hoped for at each stage?
F2. What cash, monthly gross and net burn, approved budget, payment timing, runway, and funding milestones apply under base, optimistic, adverse, and severe cases?
F3. What conditions, control rights, reporting duties, or strategic constraints accompany the funding?
F4. How concentrated are revenue and funding among customers, sponsors, investors, grants, parent organizations, regions, currencies, channels, or payment providers?
F5. When do acquisition cost, gross margin, retention, payback, and lifetime value become sustainable, and what evidence supports the assumptions?
F6. How sensitive is viability to price, demand, conversion, churn, salaries, infrastructure, support, compliance, currency, and financing cost?
F7. Which costs are fixed, variable, discretionary, contractual, or expensive to reverse, and which cost cliffs—scale tiers, licenses, hardware, insurance, audits, localization, or regulation—are missing from the normal forecast?
F8. What minimum viable team and service level preserve critical knowledge, security, compliance, support, and safety if funding or customer payment is delayed, reduced, restricted, repriced, or withdrawn?
F9. Which financial thresholds trigger hiring restraint, scope reduction, fundraising, sponsorship, a pivot, sale, merger, pause, retirement, or orderly shutdown?
F10. Is money reserved for surviving obligations such as payroll or severance, tax, debt, contracts, security incidents, legal duties, refunds, data export, customer migration, support, intellectual property protection, and decommissioning?
F11. Who owns the cash forecast and financial model, how often are actuals compared with a reforecast, and which variances require a decision?
F12. Is there a credible path to sustained operation before available funding ends?
F13. How much capacity or time is lost while waiting for budgets, procurement, finance approval, payment, reimbursement, or hiring authorization, and can reprioritization, pricing, collection terms, renegotiation, staged scope, or shared infrastructure relieve the bottleneck before adding capital?

## Success bottlenecks and failure conditions

B1. What outcome defines success, and what minimum result would still justify continued investment?
B2. What single constraint currently limits success: evidence, adoption, distribution, skills, time, capital, technology, regulation, trust, operations, or something else?
B3. Which dependency or decision lies on the critical path, and what is its latest safe resolution date?
B4. Which uncertain assumption could invalidate the product even if execution is otherwise strong?
B5. What leading signal will reveal each bottleneck before it becomes irreversible?
B6. Who owns each bottleneck, and do they have the authority, budget, information, and time to address it?
B7. What prevention, mitigation, workaround, fallback, and stop rule apply?
B8. What is the cheapest and fastest credible test of the most dangerous constraint?
B9. Which constraints are correlated and could fail together under the same event?
B10. Is every critical bottleneck recorded, reviewed at a defined cadence, and linked to a decision or action?

## Legal, compliance, and governance

11. In which jurisdictions and industries will the product operate?
12. Which laws, regulations, contracts, standards, certifications, and internal policies apply?
13. What software, content, data, model, font, and media licenses constrain use or distribution?
14. Are terms, notices, consent, warranties, disclaimers, and customer commitments accurate and consistent with behavior?
15. What records and evidence are required for audit, investigation, or dispute?
16. Which decisions require legal, security, privacy, risk, finance, or executive approval?
17. Who is accountable for product outcomes, control operation, and accepted risks?
18. How are exceptions approved, time-limited, monitored, and closed?
19. How will regulatory and contractual changes be detected and implemented?
20. Are suppliers and subprocessors assessed, contracted, monitored, and replaceable?

## Ethics and sustainability

21. Does the product respect autonomy, informed choice, dignity, and reasonable expectations?
22. Could interface design exploit attention, addiction, urgency, confusion, or power imbalance?
23. Are benefits, errors, and burdens distributed unfairly across groups?
24. Could the product enable discrimination, surveillance, censorship, labor displacement, environmental harm, or concentration of power?
25. Which affected groups have not participated in the decision?
26. Are explanations and appeal paths proportionate to the consequence of a decision?
27. What energy, hardware, network, storage, and disposal costs are material?
28. Can the same outcome be achieved with less computation, data, or consumption?
29. Which uses will be prohibited even if profitable or legal?
30. Who can stop a harmful launch or operation, and how are concerns protected?

## Measurement and learning

31. What is the primary outcome metric, and why is it a valid proxy for value?
32. What leading indicators predict that outcome?
33. Which guardrails detect harm, exclusion, gaming, poor quality, or unsustainable cost?
34. What is the baseline, target, segment, time window, and minimum meaningful change?
35. Can the metric be manipulated or improved while user outcomes worsen?
36. What qualitative evidence is needed to interpret the numbers?
37. What hypothesis, exposure rule, sample, duration, and decision threshold govern each experiment?
38. Are experiments ethical, statistically credible, and safe to stop?
39. Who reviews results and turns them into a decision?
40. Which metrics should be removed because they no longer guide action?

## Evolution and retirement

41. What is the product's versioning and compatibility policy?
42. How will users learn about and migrate through breaking changes?
43. How long will old clients, APIs, formats, and workflows be supported?
44. What telemetry and feedback justify continuation, redesign, consolidation, or removal?
45. How are experiments, flags, temporary migrations, and obsolete paths cleaned up?
46. Can users export data and move to another solution without unreasonable friction?
47. What events would trigger retirement, and who makes that decision?
48. What communication period, contractual obligation, refund, and migration assistance apply?
49. How will data be returned, retained, anonymized, or destroyed and proven complete?
50. How will infrastructure, credentials, domains, integrations, documentation, and support be safely decommissioned?
