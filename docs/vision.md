# Vision and boundaries

## Problem

Teams often discover important questions only after a costly decision has been made.
Checklists help, but long universal lists create noise, hide dependencies, and give a
false sense of completion. Documents then drift apart because the reasoning behind
them is scattered across meetings, tickets, and individual memory.

## Vision

Scrutable helps a team discover what it needs to ask before the omission becomes
expensive. It maintains a connected, evolving model of questions, answers, evidence,
decisions, risks, owners, and outputs for any software-producing effort.

## Goals

- Reveal important unknowns across the complete system lifecycle.
- Adapt the interview to the subject, risk, and previous answers.
- Recommend the next question by impact, uncertainty, and unlock value.
- Trace important decisions back to evidence and forward to tests and owners.
- Generate consistent human-readable documents and machine-readable state.
- Define honest assurance criteria that can be checked automatically.

## Non-goals

- Guarantee product-market fit, profit, delivery dates, or defect-free software.
- Replace expert judgment, user research, threat modeling, or regulatory advice.
- Force every project to answer every catalog question.
- Choose an architecture or business strategy on behalf of accountable people.
- Store project answers inside the reusable catalog.

## Intended subjects

A subject can be an application, service, feature, platform, data pipeline, internal
process, organization, or a meaningful subsystem. Every assessment must state its
boundary so that answers are neither accidentally global nor misleadingly narrow.

## Success criteria for the framework

Scrutable is succeeding when teams detect consequential omissions earlier,
produce stronger evidence for high-impact decisions, reduce contradictory documents,
and can explain why a system was considered ready at a particular moment.
