# Discovery and experience questions

## Vision, problem, and outcomes

1. What future becomes possible if this product succeeds?
2. Can the problem be described without mentioning a proposed feature?
3. Who experiences the problem most intensely and frequently?
4. What triggers the need, and what indicates the job is complete?
5. What functional, emotional, and social outcomes matter?
6. What evidence comes from observed behavior rather than stated preference?
7. What assumptions are both uncertain and capable of invalidating the idea?
8. Could a process, policy, service, or existing tool solve the problem better than new software?
9. Why is this the right time, and what could make the opportunity disappear?
10. What will we learn with the cheapest credible experiment?

## Users and stakeholders

11. Which user segments behave differently enough to need different solutions?
12. Who selects, buys, configures, uses, supports, audits, and can block the product?
13. Who is affected even though they never use the interface?
14. What knowledge, motivation, time, equipment, and authority does each role have?
15. Where do stakeholder incentives conflict?
16. What prevents adoption: trust, effort, migration, price, habit, policy, or switching cost?
17. Which vulnerable or underserved groups require explicit consideration?
18. How will users participate in discovery, design, and validation over time?

## Market and positioning

19. What are the direct, indirect, manual, and “do nothing” alternatives?
20. On which decision criteria do alternatives win or lose?
21. Is the advantage meaningful, defensible, and easy to explain?
22. Who has budget and authority to buy, and how long is the decision cycle?
23. Which distribution channel can repeatedly reach the intended users?
24. What proof is needed before a buyer or user will trust the product?
25. What market, platform, or regulatory shift could help or destroy the strategy?

## Scope and prioritization

26. What end-to-end outcome must the first release deliver?
27. Which capability is necessary, differentiating, expected, or merely attractive?
28. What will we explicitly not build in this phase?
29. Which dependencies or unknowns should be tested first?
30. What is the cost of delay for each major capability?
31. How will scope change requests be evaluated?
32. Is each roadmap item linked to an outcome, evidence, and owner?

## Experience and content

33. What are the critical journeys, including onboarding, routine work, failure recovery, support, and exit?
34. What does the user need to know, choose, provide, and confirm at each step?
35. Can the product reduce steps, choices, data entry, interruption, and cognitive load?
36. Does the interface use the user's language and mental model?
37. Are system status, consequences, uncertainty, and next actions visible?
38. Can users undo, correct, retry, resume, and safely abandon work?
39. Are empty, loading, offline, partial, error, expired, and permission-denied states designed?
40. Is help available at the point of need, with a path to a human when appropriate?
41. Are notifications necessary, timely, understandable, controllable, and non-manipulative?
42. Does the experience remain coherent across devices and channels?

## Accessibility and inclusion

43. Can all critical tasks be completed by keyboard and assistive technology?
44. Are focus, labels, structure, contrast, zoom, motion, audio, and target sizes accessible?
45. Is meaning conveyed without relying only on color, sound, shape, or position?
46. Does the product work with slow connections, older devices, and interruption?
47. Are language, date, time, number, name, address, and cultural assumptions explicit?
48. Does identity collection allow real human variation without collecting unnecessary data?
49. Have people with relevant disabilities and constraints tested the actual journey?
50. Which accessibility standard and conformance level must be met, and how will it be audited continuously?

## Functional behavior

51. What entities, rules, calculations, states, and state transitions define the domain?
52. What must always be true before and after each operation?
53. Which actions are idempotent, reversible, time-sensitive, or require confirmation?
54. What happens with duplicates, stale data, concurrent changes, partial completion, and retries?
55. Which roles may view, create, change, approve, export, or delete each resource?
56. What limits, quotas, ordering rules, and validation rules apply?
57. What audit information is required for consequential changes?
58. Are acceptance criteria observable, unambiguous, and testable?
