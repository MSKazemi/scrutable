# Enterprise implementation and customer success

## Purpose and boundary

This method covers the work between organizational purchase and sustained customer
value: configuration, migration, integration, data readiness, approvals, rollout,
change, time to first value, customer-success ownership, adoption, value realization,
renewal risk, recovery, and exit.

It does not promise that a contract, project plan, deployment, provisioned seat, or
login creates value. It does not replace customer authority, security or legal
review, professional services judgment, change management, or contractual remedies.

## Conditional operating loop

Use the eight questions in
[`catalog/business/enterprise-implementation-questions.yaml`](../catalog/business/enterprise-implementation-questions.yaml)
as one conditional loop:

1. map everything the provider, customer, partner, and supplier must configure,
   migrate, integrate, clean, approve, and change before first value;
2. assign accepted ownership, authority, due dates, evidence, and escalation to every
   cross-party dependency;
3. define time-to-first-value, milestones, acceptance evidence, and intervention
   thresholds;
4. make recurring identity, provisioning, audit, security, data-processing,
   accessibility, procurement, and policy blockers reusable product inputs;
5. confirm the operational champion, executive sponsor, administrator, support path,
   and renewal owner;
6. measure workflow adoption and value realization rather than license activation;
7. detect stalled rollout, shelfware, churn, downsell, and renewal risk early; and
8. execute a bounded recovery, rescope, rollback, or exit when the promised outcome
   is no longer achievable.

Record the result in the
[enterprise implementation register](../templates/enterprise-implementation-register.md).

## Implementation dependency model

Treat implementation as a cross-organizational system. Map at least:

- configuration, tenant or environment creation, identity, roles, provisioning, and
  access;
- integration, API, network, security, audit, privacy, legal, procurement, and
  accessibility gates;
- data discovery, ownership, extraction, cleaning, mapping, migration, validation,
  reconciliation, retention, and deletion;
- workflow design, policy, training, communications, change safeguards, parallel run,
  cutover, fallback, and support readiness;
- customer, provider, partner, supplier, regulator, and machine-evaluator
  dependencies;
- required decision, authority, evidence, capacity, due date, predecessor, successor,
  and escalation path.

A dependency is not complete because one party says its work is done. Define the
receiving party's acceptance evidence and the effect on the next value milestone.

## Cross-party ownership

Each dependency has one accountable owner with sufficient authority, a responsible
executor, consulted reviewers, informed stakeholders, accepted due date, evidence,
and escalation. Distinguish provider obligations from customer-controlled work and
joint decisions. Do not assign customer dependencies to a provider who cannot compel
them or hide provider product gaps as “customer readiness.”

Confirm deputies and continuity for critical sponsors, champions, administrators,
architects, migration owners, security reviewers, and support contacts. Record what
happens when an owner leaves, loses authority, or cannot meet the milestone.

## Time to first value and milestones

Define the first observable beneficial outcome, baseline, target population,
measurement window, and evidence. Separate milestones such as contract, kickoff,
configuration, migration, integration, training, launch, first use, accepted workflow,
first value, sustained value, and expansion.

Every milestone states:

- outcome and acceptance evidence;
- owner and receiving approver;
- planned and latest safe date;
- dependencies and critical path;
- user or operational impact;
- intervention, escalation, pause, rescope, rollback, and stop thresholds.

Activity volume, percent-complete estimates, or deployment status are not substitutes
for accepted evidence.

## Recurring rollout blockers

Analyze implementation delays, security questionnaires, lost deals, audit findings,
provisioning failures, data-processing negotiations, accessibility reviews, and
procurement exceptions across customers. Convert recurring requirements into
product capabilities, standard evidence packages, documented limits, reusable
integrations, supported migration tools, or explicit market exclusions.

Do not promise compliance, identity compatibility, auditability, accessibility, data
processing, or integration based on a sales statement. Preserve the exact customer
requirement, applicable scope, evidence, owner, expiry, and unresolved limitation.

## Customer-success roles and governance

Before operational handoff, confirm:

- an operational champion who owns the changed workflow;
- an executive sponsor with authority to remove organizational blockers;
- an administrator and technical owner with documented access and succession;
- a support path with service targets, diagnostics, privacy boundaries, and
  escalation;
- a customer outcome owner and provider customer-success owner;
- a commercial or renewal owner who can evaluate value and alternatives;
- a cadence for decisions, incidents, adoption, outcomes, risks, and changes.

A named person without authority, capacity, incentives, or continuity evidence does
not satisfy the role.

## Adoption and value realization

Measure whether intended users complete the target workflow, reduce the relevant
problem, and obtain the promised beneficial outcome. Segment by role, business unit,
location, workflow, cohort, and time. Include workarounds, shadow systems, support
burden, error, exclusion, employee impact, and unintended side effects.

License activation, provisioned accounts, logins, or feature clicks may be inputs but
are not value evidence. Preserve the customer's own baseline, success definition,
and dissent when provider and customer interpretations differ.

## Renewal and expansion risk

Leading indicators can include milestone slippage, missing owners, unresolved
blockers, low target-workflow coverage, absent value evidence, rising support demand,
workarounds, administrative inactivity, champion departure, sponsor disengagement,
contractual disputes, product gaps, incident history, budget change, and credible
alternatives.

Validate signals against outcomes and record false alarms and missed risk. Do not use
a risk score to manipulate, pressure, surveil, or bypass customer decision-makers.
Each threshold has a proportionate action, owner, latest useful intervention time,
and customer-impact safeguard.

## Recovery, rescope, and exit

Predefine bounded recovery options: additional evidence, ownership correction,
technical remediation, migration repair, workflow redesign, training, schedule or
scope change, temporary parallel operation, rollback, commercial remedy, data return,
transition assistance, suspension, and exit.

A recovery decision must state the promised outcome, remaining uncertainty, cost,
customer burden, legal and contractual implications, latest useful deadline, and stop
criteria. Do not continue because sunk cost, revenue recognition, internal forecast,
or reputation makes failure uncomfortable.

## Agent use and permissions

An agent can maintain the dependency graph and register, compare requirements,
prepare migration checks, calculate milestones and leading indicators, summarize
customer-approved evidence, and draft intervention options. It must distinguish
provider evidence from customer assertions, preserve access and confidentiality
boundaries, record uncertainty, and avoid inventing customer acceptance.

Separate explicit authorization is required before contacting a customer, changing
an implementation plan or contract, accessing customer systems or data, provisioning
or revoking access, publishing status, committing resources, issuing a commercial
remedy, or initiating exit.

## Gate conditions

A positive implementation or customer-value gate is blocked when:

- a material provider, customer, partner, data, approval, migration, or workflow
  dependency is unknown;
- a critical dependency lacks accepted ownership, authority, evidence, due date, or
  escalation;
- time to first value and milestone acceptance cannot be measured;
- a recurring security, legal, identity, accessibility, data, audit, or procurement
  blocker remains hidden or unsupported;
- critical customer-success roles are absent or lack authority and continuity;
- claimed adoption or value is inferred only from activation or usage volume;
- material stalled-rollout or renewal risk lacks a timely intervention; or
- failure has no bounded recovery, data return, transition, contractual, or exit path.

Structural completion means that the implementation and value system is explicit and
owned. It remains partial until representative implementations, failed cases,
recovery exercises, false-blocker review, and renewal outcomes calibrate the method.
